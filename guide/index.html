THE MAKING OF

Nocturne: Depths

How an AI designed, built, and shipped a twenty-level bioluminescent
boss-fight odyssey — and how you can do the same.

01 — THE BRIEFTwenty depths, a terror in every one

Nocturne: Depths was built end-to-end by Claude (Fable 5) inside Claude Code, working autonomously: concept, art direction, code, sound, iteration, and deployment. The brief — the original Nocturne, but with twenty levels and a boss in every one — so every asset here is still procedural. No stock images, no downloaded models, no sprite sheets. If you see it or hear it, it was generated by math at runtime.

The loop: you are a wisp of bioluminescence sinking through twenty named depths of the ocean. Gather motes of light until your glow wakes the thing that lives at this depth. Then your light becomes your weapon — every attack is cast from the lumens you carry, aimed at the creature's golden weak point. A new attack form unlocks every five depths, and the dash is your dodge, not your sword. Every depth is darker and stranger than the last; fell all twenty terrors and the reef blooms behind you the whole way down.

02 — PALETTEAbyssal ink, four ways to glow

Deep-sea bioluminescence is a gift of a palette: a nearly-black blue field means every emissive color reads at full intensity. The rule here was one dark, three lights — cyan for the player and the living reef, violet for depth and bloom gradients, amber for the collectible motes (warmth = reward), and a hostile crimson reserved exclusively for danger.

Because everything renders through a bloom pass, colors were authored hotter than 1.0 in the shaders (e.g. col * 1.5) so highlights overflow into halos naturally instead of being faked with sprites.

The pairing is deliberate tension: a light italic garamond feels like something written by hand underwater — fluid, organic, slightly antique — while a tracked-out grotesk gives the HUD the cold precision of a dive instrument. The big rule: the serif is never small and the grotesk is never large. Each face does one job at one scale.

04 — THE 3D TOOLBOXNine techniques, one file

Built on Three.js with no build step — an import map, one module, custom GLSL everywhere it counts:

  • Gradient sky dome — an inverted sphere with a fragment shader blending three depths of blue, plus an animated shimmer band near the "surface" far above. It follows the camera so the world never ends.
  • Volumetric god rays — seven additive planes with a sin² falloff shader, each breathing on its own clock and swaying a few degrees. Cheap, and completely convincing in fog.
  • Exponential fog — FogExp2 is the entire depth trick. Anything far dissolves into ink, which sells scale and hides the world bounds.
  • GPU marine snow — 1,200 drifting particles whose fall and sway are computed entirely in the vertex shader from a time uniform and a per-particle seed. Zero CPU cost.
  • Ribbon tendrils — the player's trailing arms are triangle-strip ribbons rebuilt each frame from a chain of positions following each other (verlet-style "follow the leader"), tapered and faded along their length by a custom attribute. You grow a new tendril every time a cluster blooms.
  • Procedural coral — each cluster merges ~40 randomized cylinders and flattened cones into a single draw call, with per-vertex height attributes driving a sway animation and a dormant→bioluminescent color ramp in GLSL. Waking a cluster just tweens one uniform.
  • Fresnel-rimmed urchins — the enemies are merged spike geometry with a rim-light shader: near-black facing you, pulsing crimson at the silhouette. Dark-on-dark, but always legible.
  • Pooled particle bursts — one 600-slot Points pool handles every collect sparkle, sting, and bloom exhale. Nothing allocates at runtime.
  • UnrealBloomPass — the glue. One post-processing pass turns every overbright pixel into light you can almost swim through, plus CSS vignette and animated film grain on top.

05 — TWENTY DEPTHSOne config object is the whole campaign

The twenty levels aren't twenty scenes — they're one world reinterpreted twenty times by a single levelConfig(n) function. Each depth gets a name ("The Sunless Garden", "Black Meridian", "Abyssal Crown"…), a summon cost, a strike cost, boss health, enemy speed, urchin count — all smooth functions of the depth number. Balance lives in five one-line formulas, so retuning the entire campaign is a one-minute edit.

The palette descends with you: three anchor palettes — teal shallows → violet vault → crimson abyss — are interpolated across the twenty depths and fed to the sky dome, fog, and god rays as uniforms, cross-fading over 2.6 seconds whenever you sink. The water itself tells you how deep you are.

Five creatures cycle through four escalating tiers each — real bodies with real behaviors, all still procedural geometry sharing one rim-light shader, every dangerous move telegraphed by a pulsing crimson ring before it lands:

  • The Dredger — a hump-backed scavenger that crawls the reef floor on six scuttling legs. Its claw rises visibly before every arc swipe, it rears back before a lunging charge (shielded while charging), and its glowing mouth on the front underside is always exposed — the boss that teaches the whole language.
  • The Reliquary — an armored heart orbited by torus-arc shell plates with one gap. Damage only lands through the gap — position yourself, or thread a projectile — except during the vent, when the whole shell flares open for three seconds before snapping shut in a shockwave. It spits bolts through its own gap: the open path is also the dangerous one.
  • The Undertow — a true eel now: elongated skull, jaw fins, dorsal plates, a tapering body driven by follow-the-leader physics. It rears back before charging, whips a telegraphed tail sweep if you hunt its back half, and at deeper tiers it burrows beneath the reef — untargetable — before erupting under a marked circle.
  • The Hollow Choir — a bell-crowned body carried on five grasping limb-chains. Limbs slam where you were standing (ring warning, shockwave on impact) and stay stuck for a moment — the only time its core is fully open. Between slams, everything you throw at it only chips.
  • The Last Lure — the finale. Phase one is the old cruelty: a nearly-invisible stalker dangling a lure pixel-identical to the motes you collect, only vulnerable when spent after a lunge. At half health it sheds the disguise: void pools bloom under your feet, bolt spirals fill the water, and a screaming shockwave cycle leaves it exhausted and open. All four attacks matter here — pulse clears the bolt screens, projectiles chip through the cast phase, the tether burns the exhaust window, and a full void charge is the hardest single answer it ever receives.

The system keeps the original game's DNA: light is still the only currency. Motes are ammunition for every attack, and getting stung scatters your lumens back into the water.

06 — THE FOUR LIGHTSA new attack every five depths

Combat is a progressive kit. Attacks are selected with the 1–4 keys (or Q/E to cycle, or clicking the HUD slots), each with its own independent cooldown — switching never resets anything. Locked slots stay visible in the HUD with the depth numeral where they wake. The dash (space or right-click) is pure movement now: a dodge with a flicker of grace frames, never a weapon.

  • I · Lumen Pulse — a fast close-range burst ring around the wisp. Cheap, reliable, and it detonates incoming bolts, which quietly makes it the defensive tool you'll still be using at depth twenty.
  • VI · Lumen Projectile — a precise cursor-aimed dart, fired on the move. The safe answer, and the only way to thread the Reliquary's gap from across the arena.
  • XI · Lumen Tether — a living beam bound to the weak point. Highest sustained damage in the game, but it drains your lumens while held and snaps if you're hit, drift too far, or the weak point closes. Risk, priced honestly.
  • XVI · Void Charge — hold to gather, release a piercing beam across the whole arena. Charging anchors you nearly in place: the strongest hit in the game costs you your mobility for two full seconds, and pain scatters the gathering void.

Nothing obsoletes anything. The pulse clears screens the beam can't, the projectile is the only safe chip against armored phases, the tether out-damages everything when you can hold it, and the charge rewards the one skill the rest of the game builds: reading the telegraphs well enough to know when you have two seconds to stand still.

07 — SOUNDAn instrument, not a soundtrack

There are no audio files. The entire soundscape is a small WebAudio synthesizer built at the moment you press DESCEND:

  • An abyssal pad — four detuned sine oscillators (A1, E2, A2, E3) through a lowpass filter whose cutoff breathes on a 16-second LFO, plus looped filtered noise for the water itself.
  • Collect chimes — a pentatonic scale that climbs as your combo grows, each note doubled by a quiet shimmer partial one octave up, fed into a feedback delay for that cathedral-underwater tail.
  • Bloom chords — an arpeggiated C–G–C–E–G swell with a deep root when a cluster wakes.
  • Urchin thud — a sine pitch-dropping from 86Hz to 34Hz. Felt more than heard.
  • Boss roar — a sawtooth diving from 70Hz to 26Hz through a closing lowpass filter: the sound of something very large noticing you.
  • The dread layer — a 41Hz triangle wave pulsing at 1.1Hz that fades in only during boss fights and out the moment they end. You stop noticing it until it's gone.
  • Strike & dash — a bright triangle clang with a pitch-drop thump for landed strikes; a bandpass noise sweep rising 500→2400Hz for the dash whoosh.
  • Four attack voices — the pulse is a low sine drop under a soft noise bloom, the projectile a descending triangle chirp, the tether a tremolo dyad that hums only while the bind holds, and the void charge a sawtooth that climbs for two seconds and detonates into filtered noise. You can tell what the player is doing with your eyes closed.

08 — ITERATIONPasses with a fine-toothed comb

The first build is never the shipped build. Depths went through three deliberate review passes, each verified with scripted headless-browser playthroughs — real mouse movement, real dashes aimed from the game's own state — plus screenshots of every archetype at depths 1, 5, 10, 15 and 20:

PASS ONE — SCALE & SPECTACLE

Screenshotting every boss revealed two failures of scale. The player's halo grew with every carried lumen, uncapped — by 100 lumens it was a fog bank swallowing a third of the screen (capped at 18 lumens' worth). And bosses spawned 20 units away with cruise-speed pursuit, so the Maw and the Angler never even reached the frame — fixed with a 3.5× "entrance boost" that decays once they close within striking distance. Menace needs to arrive.

PASS TWO — COMBAT PROOF

Not screenshots — proof. A script read the boss's weak-point position from a debug hook, converted it to screen space, and landed real aimed dashes on the level-1 Leviathan: three strikes, three lumens each, boss down. Then a full campaign run validated all twenty depths advance, the five vulnerability patterns behave (the Maw only when open, the Queen only after teleporting, the Angler only when exhausted), and the win screen fires at depth twenty. Zero console errors across the entire descent.

THE COMBAT REDESIGN

The original strike — dashing bodily into the boss — was one verb pretending to be a combat system. It was replaced wholesale: numeric boss health, a positional damageMul(attackerPos) gate so armor gaps and phase windows govern every attack identically, and the four-light kit above. The rebuild was verified the same way it was built: a script that plays with real mouse and keyboard input confirmed dashing into a boss now deals exactly zero damage, each of the four attacks kills or wounds a live boss, locked attacks refuse to select at every threshold (1, 5, 6, 10, 11, 15, 16, 20), and the pulse-clear-then-charge rotation the late bosses demand actually works — three full beams landed once the bolt screen was cleared first.

PASS THREE — PACING & DRAMA

The campaign run exposed a rhythm flaw: leftover lumens above the next summon cost woke the next terror instantly, deleting the calm between fights. Now every depth begins with a mandatory three-second breath — announcement card, palette cross-fade, silence — before anything can wake. Add the hitstop slow-motion on every landed strike, the 41Hz dread-layer that fades in only during fights, and the shockwave ring when a terror dissolves, and each depth gets a complete dramatic arc: calm, dread, violence, bloom.

09 — SHIP ITDeploying to Netlify

The whole game is static files — no build step, no server — which makes deployment nearly instant:

nocturne-depths/
├── index.html        ← the game
├── css/style.css
├── js/main.js        ← all 3D, 20 levels, 5 bosses & audio
├── guide/index.html  ← this page
└── netlify.toml
# one-time
npm i -g netlify-cli
netlify login

# from the project folder
netlify deploy --prod --dir .

That's it. Netlify serves guide/index.html at /guide automatically. Three.js loads from a CDN via an import map, fonts from Google Fonts — everything else in this project is generated the moment your browser opens it.

10 — STEAL THIS WORKFLOWThe recipe

  • Pick a world before a game. "Deep-sea bioluminescence" decided the palette, audio, typography and mechanics in one stroke.
  • One dark, few lights. Emissive colors on a near-black field + a bloom pass gives you 90% of "otherworldly" for free.
  • Author brightness above 1.0 and let post-processing make the halos.
  • Make the verbs feel good first — springy motion, magnetic pickups, sounds that climb with success.
  • Procedural everything — shaders, merged geometry and WebAudio keep the site one deploy, zero assets, infinitely tweakable.
  • Scale content with formulas, not files. Twenty levels from one config function means the campaign is retunable in minutes, and every level is guaranteed to share the game's DNA.
  • Build bosses from the player's own vocabulary. Every boss remixes mechanics the player already knows — motes, dashing, the urchin silhouette — so each fight is a twist, not a tutorial.
  • Iterate like an editor, not a builder. Three passes: legibility, feel, drama — in that order.
